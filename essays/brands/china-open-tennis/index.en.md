*Geng Yue · Brand Stories*

Beijing's sky, these days, has been beautiful — so blue.

After a September of back-to-back work, my spirit was worn to the bone. Yet the moment the plane touched down in Beijing late that night, **that old vitality rekindled in me, as if on its own.**

Perhaps that is the city's "charm" — or its "devilry."

You must summon courage from thin air, reignite your fighting spirit. You want to experience the unknown; you care about curiosity and growth.

What I want to share with you is this: I went and experienced something I never imagined. **As someone who isn't a sports fan, I spent a day at the China Open — Asia's premier tennis tournament.**

In my ideal picture of the city, this is the color Beijing should have. A crisp blue. A fearless blue. A precious blue.

The day we went happened to be the 15th anniversary of the China Open.

In fact, every year, many of the people who come to the tournament are not tennis fans — not sports fans at all.

So let me tell you, from my perspective, **how an ordinary person experiences the charm of a day that feels like a journey through sporting elegance.**

![China Open through an ordinary lens](images/011.jpg)

**Every thwack and every breath tugs at your heartbeat.**

Every slight shift of the feet is the accumulation of tens of thousands of repetitions.

Every swing of the racket demands split-second judgment from the brain and the body's full range of abilities to support it.

![Details of tennis in motion](images/012.jpg)

The afternoon we watched, Wang Yafan battled the defending champion Garcia for three grueling hours.

Both players threw everything they had at every single ball. Their sheer willpower carried a force that seemed to **"strive with all its might to break through the whole universe."**

I stood in awe of that "strength." And I ached at the vulnerability, the exhaustion, the struggle, and the surrender that showed through so rawly.

After all, these women had fought on courts around the world; many of them were playing through injuries.

![Player's iron will](images/013.jpg)

![Vulnerability and perseverance](images/014.jpg)

**"Pain."**

That was what Simona Halep, the world No. 1, said.

She played through a back injury for the first set right in front of us, then had to retire from the match.

Gain or loss, laughter or tears — victory, defeat, and struggle are the ordinary stuff of sport.

But I will remember these moments, one by one. Because **when you sit there and witness it in person, the story means something different.**

![Halep's retirement moment](images/015.jpg)

/ Khachanov, Ćorić...

/ Del Potro and Zverev taking the doubles court...

From a fan's perspective, watching in person **is intensely satisfying.**

/ No two tennis matches in the world are exactly the same — **the details of every match differ.**

/ You can see the player's full-body movement from every angle.

/ You can see clearly how each point was won. **The sting of an unforced error, the surge of a comeback** — both are experienced at maximum intensity.

![In-person viewing perspective](images/017.jpg)

/ Watching live, **your senses are more thrillingly engaged.** The passion and cheers of the fans let you feel the love everyone has for the sport of tennis.

/ Sitting in the court, if your seat happens to be dead center, you track the ball left and right, intimately feeling **the texture and pace of a groundstroke.**

I had a VIP ticket, but ever the reader-minded soul, I asked about the affordable ticket prices for you:

**Huanxiang tickets are only 30 yuan each**, letting you **watch all day** at the National Tennis Center — an extraordinary **value.**

If you feel the outer courts haven't quite satisfied you and you want to see the top-tier matchups,

just head to the Liangting upgrade box office and **enter the Diamond Court to experience the most intense, most electrifying sports competition.**

![Diamond Court](images/023.jpg)

The **Diamond Court** we sat in that day is the only stadium in Asia with a double-decker skybox.

The **roof** left a marvelous impression on me.

![Beijing sky](images/003.jpg)

![Diamond Court roof](images/024.jpg)

🌞 Under clear skies, the roof opens, and everyone inside can look up and see blue sky and white clouds directly overhead.

☁ In foul weather, the roof closes within 12 minutes, transforming the space into a bright, enclosed tennis "arena."

This is the main stadium, seating ten thousand fans.

Since I went early, the crowd was still modest that day. A venue this outstanding — I sincerely hope it will be packed to the rafters in years to come.

![Inside the court](images/025.jpg)

To have experienced all of this so fully, I'm grateful to the China Open for inviting us to try out the VIP vantage of the "**skyboxes**."

After a day of VIP treatment, let me highlight for you what makes it expensive (and excellent):

**Privacy** /

There is an indoor lounge of over 30 square meters, fully equipped for eating, drinking, and relaxing.

Comfortable sofas and a spread of snacks and beverages.

A private restroom, plus two volunteers on site all day.

**Freedom** /

Once you purchase a skybox, the owner can arrange the viewing space however they wish — fully satisfying every desire for watching the match.

The interior design can be DIY'd by the purchaser.

**View** /

There are 18 dedicated outdoor balcony seats with zero blind spots.

"Each suite also has an LCD screen providing a live broadcast feed synchronized with the match.

If you'd rather watch from inside the room, that's fine too."

When we walked out of the venue, the lights came on. I saw Beijing's signature red and blue.

I remember years ago, when I first ventured to Beijing on my own — it was autumn too. What I remember is the vermilion of the city walls and the tile-blue of the sky.

Those two colors have never changed. Beijing is still Beijing, and dreamers still come one after another. I wish every young person well, and I am no longer that youth.

But what draws me to this city is the same as today. The world's top players and competitions, places that are always fun, novel experiences, stimulating and substantial information, people you can talk to about dreams and the future.

The title of this piece comes from a song I heard recently — one that burns.

We stay in Beijing, we endure this city — why? At this moment, this is the answer.

**Wherever my dream is, that's where I drift.**
