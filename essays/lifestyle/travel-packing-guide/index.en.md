### Power, Internet, Money

There are many things to bring, but my **first principle** is: make sure you have power, internet, and money.

- Your phone must have battery.
- WiFi must connect / data must flow.
- Your wallet must have local currency and an international credit card.

**Power**: I bring a universal adapter (I have one that combines Europe, US, and Southeast Asia, plus separate ones too).

**Internet**: Beyond communication and browsing, phone map navigation is essential abroad. I usually have three or four options:

- Rent a portable WiFi for the destination country at the airport in advance
- Buy a local SIM card or data plan upon arrival
- Call China Mobile to activate a destination-country data package, the kind with unlimited daily data for a few dozen yuan
- Huawei phones have SkyTone for purchasing overseas data

**Yue Note**: Because I've lost things in various countries, my advice: **Important documents** — passport, visa, travel itinerary, accommodation address, and key contact info — save a scanned PDF on your phone / email an electronic copy to yourself / print two hard copies, one in your suitcase and one in your carry-on. With all this prepared, even if problems arise, **you'll stay fully connected**.

### The In-Flight Pouch

There was a time when I would say, "The airport is my home." I often flew over ten hours, landing and starting work shortly after. So I needed to make myself comfortable on the flight.

After security, I pull out a small cloth pouch and fill it with:

- **U-shaped pillow**: I use a latex one — it lets me sleep on long-haul flights and protects my neck.
- **Noise-canceling headphones**: I use Bose QC20, absolutely love them.
- Kao **steam eye mask** or a regular eye mask
- 100ml **skincare liquids**
- **Sleep aid** for jet lag, or melatonin
- A pair of **soft slippers** and soft **socks** — for overnight long-haul flights, it makes a real difference.
- Fully charged **laptop, iPad, and Kindle** — I need to document my travels and love reading on planes.

Some planes blast the AC, so remember to ask for a **blanket**. I have a quick-dry wrap that doubles as a light blanket.

### Suitcase

I have a 28-inch large suitcase and a 20-inch carry-on. I choose based on the length of the trip.

For the past few years, I've been using 90Fen suitcases — affordable, great-looking, and high quality. Their new models are pretty sleek too.

![90Fen suitcase](images/031.jpg)

![90Fen suitcase](images/032.jpg)

**Packing sequence**: There's a saying — pack like a crow dropping stones into a bottle. Put the big stones in first, then the smaller pebbles, and finally fill the cracks and corners with the fine grains.

**So: pack the large and heavy items first, then medium, then small. Tuck little things into the gaps and corners.** Best to weigh it — checked luggage shouldn't exceed 23 kg.

![Backpack](images/033.jpg)

![Backpack](images/034.jpg)

I bought this backpack on a whim while shopping with Haina in San Francisco, on that trip to Airbnb HQ. I never expected to use it for so long. It has many compartments, a huge capacity, and even helps reduce shoulder strain.

**If you love shopping**, **pack a medium suitcase inside your large one when you depart.** On the return trip, just check both. Or leave half your suitcase empty when packing, reserved for shopping.
